Online Earning Scams in India: How to Spot and Report Them

A job or task offer can look convincing and still be fraudulent. Learn the warning signs, verify offers before sharing information, and act quickly if money or account access is at risk.

Last reviewed: 30 October 2026 · Estimated reading time: 6–8 minutes

Remember: A promise of high earnings for very little work, combined with an upfront payment or pressure to act fast, is a reason to stop and verify. The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre has specifically warned about fake CAPTCHA-filling job schemes that use these tactics.

Common warning signs

  • You are told to pay a registration, training, software, verification or “refundable” fee before you can earn.
  • The offer promises unusually high or guaranteed income for simple work, with little explanation of the actual duties.
  • The recruiter pressures you to decide immediately or says you must pay to unlock a task, account or withdrawal.
  • You are asked to share an OTP, UPI PIN, card PIN, banking password or screen-sharing access.
  • The company or recruiter cannot be verified through contact details on the employer’s official website.
  • You are asked to receive, transfer or forward money through your personal bank account or wallet.
  • A displayed balance grows, but you must pay more money before the platform will release it.

One documented pattern: fake CAPTCHA jobs

An official I4C advisory describes fraudsters advertising high-paying CAPTCHA-filling work, asking for registration, training or software fees, then withholding payment and demanding additional charges. The advisory identifies upfront payment requests, unrealistic earnings and delayed or denied withdrawals as warning signs.

This advisory describes one scam pattern. Other fraudulent offers may use different stories, so verify the employer and payment terms even when the task looks different.

How to check an earning or job offer

  1. Find the source independently. Go to the company’s official website yourself. Do not rely only on a forwarded message, logo, screenshot or link supplied by a recruiter.
  2. Confirm the exact offer. Search the company’s careers page or contact its published phone number or email. Verify the job title, job ID, recruiter and application process.
  3. Read the work and payment terms. Look for duties, pay calculation, schedule, payout dates, conditions and who is responsible for fees.
  4. Do not pay to release earnings. An unexpected “tax,” “processing,” “membership” or “withdrawal” payment demanded by a task platform is a serious red flag.
  5. Protect private information. Never disclose an OTP, UPI PIN or password. Share identity documents only with a verified organisation through an appropriate secure process.
  6. Pause when pressured. A genuine opportunity should withstand the time needed for you to check it.

Check claims involving the National Career Service

The National Career Service (NCS) says its registration, job application and interview processing services are free for jobseekers. It warns that some people and websites falsely claim an association with NCS or use its logo. Verify claims at ncs.gov.in, or call the NCS helpline at 1514 to check a communication.

If you already sent money or shared details

  1. Stop sending money, codes or documents. Do not pay an additional amount to recover an earlier payment.
  2. If bank or payment details were exposed, contact your bank or payment provider promptly using its official app or website and ask how to secure the account or transaction.
  3. For financial cyber fraud, call India’s helpline at 1930 as soon as possible and report the incident at the National Cyber Crime Reporting Portal ↗.
  4. Save the phone numbers, profile names, URLs, messages, emails, receipts and transaction references. Do not delete evidence that may be useful to the report.

Reporting quickly helps authorities and payment providers receive information promptly, but it cannot guarantee that money will be recovered. Follow instructions from your bank and the official portal.

What to do when you receive a suspicious offer

  • Do not click unknown payment links or install remote-access apps.
  • Do not forward the offer as a verified vacancy.
  • Keep a screenshot and the original message or web address.
  • Verify the organisation through a known official channel.
  • Report suspected financial cyber fraud through the official route above if money was transferred.

Frequently asked questions

Are all online earning offers scams?

No. But verify the organisation, duties and payment terms before sharing information or investing time. A professional-looking page is not enough to prove legitimacy.

Should I pay a registration fee to get a job?

Treat a fee demanded by an employer or recruiter to apply, interview or secure a job as a serious warning sign. NCS says jobseekers should not pay fees to employers and that its application process is free.

What number should I call if I transferred money?

For financial cyber fraud in India, call 1930 promptly, contact your bank or payment provider, and report through cybercrime.gov.in.

Can I get my money back after reporting?

There is no guarantee. Report quickly, keep evidence and follow instructions from your bank, payment provider and the official authorities.

Keep this short rule

Verify first, never share your payment codes, and do not pay extra money to unlock a promised income. If you have already lost money, contact your bank and report it promptly through official channels.
