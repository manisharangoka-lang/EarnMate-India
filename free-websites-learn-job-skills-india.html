5 Free Websites to Learn Job Skills in India

Build practical skills with government and provider learning platforms. Compare what each site offers, understand when certificates or exams may cost extra, and choose a path that fits a real job goal.

Last reviewed: 30 October 2026 · Estimated reading time: 6–8 minutes

You do not need to pay for an expensive course to start learning. These five platforms offer free learning options in different subjects. A free course does not always mean every certificate, exam, lab or service is free, so check the individual course page before enrolling.

Good starting point: Choose a skill you see in real job descriptions, complete one relevant course, and make a small project that shows what you learned. A certificate can document study, but it does not guarantee a job.

1. Skill India Digital Hub

Skill India Digital Hub (SIDH) is a Government of India platform for discovering skill courses and related opportunities. Its catalog covers areas such as digital skills, AI, data, cybersecurity, marketing and more. Some courses are marked free, and course details can include language, duration, assessment and completion certificate information.

How to use it: Search the catalog for a role-related skill, open the course detail page and confirm the fee, eligibility, language and certificate terms shown there. Do not assume every course on the hub is free.

2. SWAYAM

SWAYAM is a Government of India online course platform with subjects from school and higher education through professional learning. The platform’s FAQ says the courses are available free to learners. If you want a SWAYAM certificate, the final proctored exam is fee-based; eligibility and certificate conditions appear on the specific course page.

How to use it: Browse by subject and course dates. Check whether the course has an optional exam, where it will be held and what the course instructor requires before relying on it for credit or certification.

3. NPTEL

NPTEL is a Ministry of Education initiative by the IITs and IISc, delivered online through SWAYAM. It offers structured courses in technical and academic subjects. NPTEL states that enrolment and learning are free. Its current information lists an optional in-person proctored certification exam at ₹1,000 per course; check the course’s own page for current dates and terms.

How to use it: Choose a course that matches your foundation and available study time. Many offerings run in set sessions, so check enrolment and exam deadlines before beginning.

4. Microsoft Learn

Microsoft Learn provides self-paced training in Microsoft products and related skills. Microsoft says its training is free. Learning modules can include knowledge checks and achievements; these are different from professional certification credentials or exams. Some practical cloud exercises may require Azure access, so review the exercise requirements first.

How to use it: Start with beginner modules in a skill area such as Excel, Power BI, cloud fundamentals, security or software development. Save progress in a Learn profile if you want to track completed modules.

5. IBM SkillsBuild

IBM SkillsBuild offers learning in AI, cybersecurity, data and other technology topics for adult learners, university students and high school learners. IBM describes the program as free. Some courses or learning paths include digital credentials; the required modules and assessment are specific to each credential.

How to use it: Pick a foundational course or learning path, note the intended audience and language, and check what is required to earn any digital credential.

How to choose the right learning site

  1. Pick a job or role you are interested in.
  2. Review several genuine job descriptions and note the skills they request.
  3. Choose one course that teaches a skill you can practise.
  4. Check the course level, language, device needs, dates and any assessment fee.
  5. Complete a small project and save it as evidence of your learning.
  6. Add the course to your resume only after you have completed it, and describe your skill level accurately.

A simple 30-day learning plan

  • Days 1–3: Choose one role and list the skills it commonly requires.
  • Days 4–18: Work through one beginner course at a steady pace and take notes.
  • Days 19–25: Build a small sample project using the skill.
  • Days 26–30: Review the project, write a short explanation of your process and update your resume or portfolio.

This is a suggested schedule, not a promise of job readiness. Some fields require longer practice, formal education or regulated qualifications.

Frequently asked questions

Are these websites completely free?

They provide free learning options, but exams, certificates, labs or some individual courses may have separate costs. Check the specific course page before registering.

Will a free course certificate get me a job?

No certificate can guarantee a job. Employers may also look for practical skills, work samples, communication and role-specific qualifications.

Can I learn only on a phone?

Some videos and reading work on a phone, but course exercises, spreadsheets, coding and exams may need a computer. Check device requirements first.

How many courses should I take?

Start with one course that matches a real goal. Applying the skill in a sample project can be more useful than collecting many unfinished courses.

Final recommendation

Use SIDH or SWAYAM to explore broad learning options, NPTEL for structured technical study, and Microsoft Learn or IBM SkillsBuild for provider-led technology training. Verify course and credential terms before enrolling, then practise what you learn.
